Competitive Landscape
No single product currently captures nitrogen at point-source, returns it as fertiliser, and provides IoT feedback — all passively, without power or service visits.
01 — What already exists
Standard Rubber Trough Mats
Existing Hardware · NZ Market
Anti-slip mats placed around troughs for hoof comfort. No nitrogen management function. Widely used on Canterbury dairy farms.
No N capture No sensor Hoof comfort
Waikato Milking Systems
Farm Equipment · NZ
Trough design and management advice for dairy farms. Optimises water access and cow flow but does not address urine nitrogen at the trough area.
No N capture No cartridge Cow flow design
Biochar Soil Amendments
Agricultural Input · Global
Bulk biochar sold as a soil amendment to improve water retention and sequester carbon. Proven NH₄⁺ adsorption (6 mg/g NO₃⁻-N). Applied broadly — not at point-source.
Biochar only No point-capture Proven chemistry
ECan Groundwater Monitoring
Regulatory · Canterbury
300+ wells monitoring nitrate levels across Canterbury. Data is available but lags 10–30 years behind farm-level action. Measures the outcome, not the source.
No intervention Data available Regulatory baseline
DairyNZ Plantain Programme
Agronomy · NZ
Plantain-based pasture mixes shown to reduce urinary nitrogen by up to 60%. Requires whole-paddock resowing. Does not address holding yards or trough areas.
Up to 60% N reduction Whole-paddock only No hardware
European Nitrogen Filter Systems
Research Stage · EU
Academic research into constructed wetlands and reed-bed filters for farm runoff. Expensive civil engineering, designed for effluent streams — not individual trough zones.
High cost Civil works required Proven filtration
02 — What CowPad borrowed from (and improved)
Layer 1 · Hardware
Bounty Gel (Verdesian)
Inspiration ↓
Hydrogel that absorbs 150× its weight in water. CowPad uses biochar instead — same absorbent logic, but biochar also adsorbs NH₄⁺ ions chemically, not just physically. Result: the spent cartridge is slow-release fertiliser, not waste water.
Layer 2 · IoT Sensor
TrapView (Pessl Instruments)
Inspiration ↓
Insect trap monitors with IoT sensors. Threshold exceeded → alert → service visit. CowPad applies the same logic: EC sensor reads biochar saturation → LED alert → cartridge swap. Proven model, new domain.
Layer 3 · GIS Platform
FBN / Farmers Business Network
Inspiration ↓
US-based satellite + data platform for farm decision-making. CowPad uses the same spatial logic with ECan well data + Canterbury-specific inputs to map nitrogen risk zones and identify where CowPad deployment matters most.
Biochar NH₄⁺ adsorption: published. EC saturation sensing: published. GIS risk mapping: existing. The missing piece is point-source hardware that combines them at trough scale — designed to fit any round trough, replace in under 30 seconds, and return the captured nitrogen to soil.
