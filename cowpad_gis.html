Why Selwyn? Real data answer
Selwyn zone has the highest mean nitrate of any Canterbury subregion: 7.43 mg/L avg, with 15 wells exceeding the safe limit.

The ECan LAWA dataset shows no improvement over 20 years. CowPad targets trough-area point-source loading — the upstream intervention before nitrogen reaches the aquifer.

How Satellite Identifies the 4 High-N Zones
Satellites cannot directly measure mg/L. But each of these four zones has a distinct ground signature — combined with LAWA well data, we can infer where nitrogen exceedance probability is highest.
① LANEWAYS Highest N
Satellite signature: Narrow bare-soil strips (3–6 m wide) connecting paddocks to the dairy shed — NDVI ≈ 0, clearly visible as linear compacted tracks
N signal: Abnormally green grass on both sides (NDVI >0.8) = N-luxury uptake = urine N exceeding pasture absorption capacity
Exceedance inference: LAWA wells at laneway ends consistently >8 mg/L → laneway N contribution confirmed
Literature: Chikazhe et al. 2023 — laneway N concentrations exceed trough zones
CowPad module: Module B straight tiles · V2 target
② HOLDING YARD Very High N
Satellite signature: Large rectangular bare-soil pad adjacent to the milking shed — dairy shed building + hardstand waiting area directly identifiable from satellite imagery
N signal: Cows wait 30–60 min before milking, urine highly concentrated; surrounding pasture shows very high CCCI (chlorophyll content index)
Exceedance inference: Hardstand runoff infiltrates adjacent soil → nearby LAWA well N levels directly reflect this loading
CowPad module: Module D large-format paving · V3 target
③ SHADE AREAS High N · Seasonal
Satellite signature: Shelter belts / tree rows visible in Canterbury imagery; irregular bare patches under canopy = summer congregation and compaction
N signal: Summer (Dec–Feb) imagery most effective; partial canopy occlusion but N-luxury grass ring visible at canopy edge
Limitation: Canopy blocks ground view — requires multi-temporal summer comparison; free satellite providers (FBN) update periodically
CowPad module: Module D portable paving · V3
④ WATER TROUGHS High N · Point Source
Satellite signature: Circular or rectangular bare-soil patches (10–30 m diameter), identifiable at 3–5 m resolution; centre of centre-pivot irrigation circles often coincides with trough location
N signal: Bare zone (NDVI ≈ 0) + surrounding 1–3 m high-NDVI grass ring = urine N point-source signature
CCCI: Elevated chlorophyll content at grass ring — N directly visible in leaf tissue
CowPad module: Module A arc tiles + B/C · V1 current target
Vegetation Indices — Precision Agriculture Reference
Index Range Agricultural meaning
NDVI 0.0–0.15 Bare / hardstand — laneways and trough congregation zones
NDVI 0.15–0.4 Sparse / stressed — compaction transition zone
NDVI 0.4–0.7 Normal pasture growth
NDVI 0.7–0.9 N-luxury — urine N exceeds pasture uptake capacity, high leaching risk
MSAVI low Use instead of NDVI where bare soil dominates (more accurate for sparse vegetation)
CCCI high Leaf chlorophyll content = direct foliar N indicator; detects N excess earlier than NDVI
Slope Risk — N Leaching Pathway
▪ 0–2° Canterbury Plains dominant — subsurface leaching, urine N travels directly to groundwater
▪ 2–8° Moderate risk — surface runoff and subsurface leaching combined
▪ >8° High surface runoff — N leaves the farm rapidly via watercourses
Canterbury Plains is predominantly 0–2° → subsurface leaching dominates → LAWA well data directly reflects farm N loading
